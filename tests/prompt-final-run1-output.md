## 1. Summary

Child A is five years old and has difficulties with handwriting, mealtimes, and loud places. The child holds pencils with a fist, tires quickly when drawing, and has trouble with buttons and zippers. The child is sensitive to messy textures and loud sounds, but seeks deep pressure and moves by spinning or rocking. The therapist recommends weekly clinic sessions alongside daily home practice to work on pencil grasp, texture tolerance, and noise tolerance.

---

## 2. Clinical terms

* **Fine motor**
* Report quote: "Fine motor: Delay noted."
* Simple explanation: The ability to use small muscles in the hands and fingers to do tasks like holding pencils, fastening buttons, and opening zippers.


* **Fist grasp**
* Report quote: "Child holds a pencil with a fist grasp"
* Simple explanation: Gripping a writing tool tightly with the whole closed hand rather than using the fingers.


* **Sensory processing**
* Report quote: "Sensory processing: Tactile defensiveness observed."
* Simple explanation: How the brain takes in, organizes, and responds to information from the senses like touch, sound, and movement.


* **Tactile defensiveness**
* Report quote: "Tactile defensiveness observed."
* Simple explanation: Being extra sensitive or uncomfortable when touching certain everyday textures, such as sticky, wet, or gritty surfaces.


* **Deep pressure**
* Report quote: "Seeks deep pressure, for example squeezing into tight spaces."
* Simple explanation: A firm, steady touch, squeeze, or hug that gives sensory input to the body's muscles and joints.


* **Vestibular regulation**
* Report quote: "Vestibular regulation: Needs support."
* Simple explanation: The body's sense of balance, motion, and position in space, controlled by the inner ear.

## 3. Five home activities

### Activity 1: Large Paper Crayon Marks

* **Title**: Large Paper Crayon Marks
* **Basis**: Goal
* **Report quote**: "Improve pencil grasp"
* **Triggers checked**:
* Difficulty with handwriting / pencil grasp: Practiced directly with thick drawing tools.
* Tires after drawing a few lines: Kept to short, single-stroke marks without pressure to draw full pictures.
* Struggles with buttons and zips: Not involved.
* Tactile defensiveness / messy textures (paint, sand): Not involved; uses clean, dry paper and wax crayons.
* Loud settings: Not involved; conducted in a quiet room.
* Unsettled when feet leave the ground: Child sits on the floor or a sturdy chair with both feet flat on the floor.


* **Materials**: One sheet of plain paper, one thick wax crayon, one drawn picture card showing a crayon.
* **Duration**: 2 to 3 minutes.
* **Steps**:
1. Place the sheet of paper and thick crayon on a low table, show the picture card of the crayon, and invite the child to sit down if they want to participate. The child may point to the card to show they are ready.
2. Model holding the thick crayon with fingers near the tip, then hand the crayon to the child to hold.
3. Encourage the child to make two or three gentle scribbles or lines across the paper.


* **Adult supervision needed**: Yes
* **Safety note**: The crayon is small enough to be put in the mouth, so watch closely to prevent mouthing or choking.
* **Therapist reminder**: Check with your therapist before trying this.
* **When to stop**: Stop immediately at the first sign of tiredness or distress, or when the child drops the crayon or turns away.

### Activity 2: Dry Fabric Touch Exploration

* **Title**: Dry Fabric Touch Exploration
* **Basis**: Goal
* **Report quote**: "tolerate a wider range of textures"
* **Triggers checked**:
* Messy textures (paint, sand): Not involved; uses completely dry, non-messy household fabrics.
* Difficulty with handwriting / fist grasp: Not involved.
* Struggles with buttons and zips: Not involved.
* Loud settings: Not involved; quiet environment.
* Unsettled when feet leave the ground: Child remains seated firmly on the floor.


* **Materials**: One smooth cotton tea towel, one knitted wool scarf, one drawn picture card showing a hand touching cloth.
* **Duration**: 2 to 3 minutes.
* **Steps**:
1. Lay the smooth cotton tea towel and knitted wool scarf flat on the floor, show the picture card of a hand touching cloth, and let the child choose whether to come near. The child may point to the fabric they want to look at.
2. Gently touch the edge of the cotton tea towel with your own fingertips, allowing the child to watch without requiring them to touch.
3. If willing, the child may touch the surface of the cotton tea towel with one fingertip, or point to the knitted scarf to explore that texture next.


* **Adult supervision needed**: Yes
* **Safety note**: The long knitted scarf poses an entanglement risk; keep it laid flat on the floor and do not drape it around the neck.
* **Therapist reminder**: Check with your therapist before trying this.
* **When to stop**: Stop immediately if the child pulls their hand away, shows distress, or turns away.

### Activity 3: Sound-Matching with Kitchen Spoons

* **Title**: Sound-Matching with Kitchen Spoons
* **Basis**: Goal
* **Report quote**: "build tolerance for noise"
* **Triggers checked**:
* Loud settings (canteens): Avoided entirely by producing very soft, predictable tapping sounds in a calm, familiar room.
* Messy textures (paint, sand): Not involved.
* Fist grasp / handwriting: Not involved.
* Struggles with buttons and zips: Not involved.
* Unsettled when feet leave the ground: Child stays seated with feet flat on the floor.


* **Materials**: Two wooden cooking spoons, one drawn picture card showing two spoons tapping.
* **Duration**: 2 to 3 minutes.
* **Steps**:
1. Place the two wooden cooking spoons on a table, present the picture card showing two spoons tapping, and allow the child to decide if they wish to approach. The child may nod or point to the card to agree.
2. Gently tap one wooden spoon against the other to produce a very quiet, soft click, showing the child where the sound comes from.
3. Offer one wooden spoon to the child so they can tap it softly against your spoon at their own pace, or point to indicate they want you to tap once more.


* **Adult supervision needed**: Yes
* **Safety note**: Wooden spoons have rigid edges that can hit the eyes or face; ensure the child taps them gently in front of their body.
* **Therapist reminder**: Check with your therapist before trying this.
* **When to stop**: Stop immediately at the first sign of distress, covering of the ears, or turning away.

### Activity 4: Large Button Board Practice

* **Title**: Large Button Board Practice
* **Basis**: Observation
* **Report quote**: "Struggles with buttons and zips"
* **Triggers checked**:
* Struggles with buttons and zips: Practiced directly using large, loose garment openings.
* Fist grasp / pencil fatigue: Not involved; uses isolated finger grasping rather than sustained drawing.
* Messy textures (paint, sand): Not involved; clean, dry fabric.
* Loud settings: Not involved; quiet room.
* Unsettled when feet leave the ground: Child sits comfortably with both feet flat on the floor.


* **Materials**: One adult cardigan with large buttons (at least 3 cm across), one drawn picture card showing a button.
* **Duration**: 3 to 4 minutes.
* **Steps**:
1. Lay the cardigan flat on a table with one large button partially slipped through its hole, show the picture card of a button, and invite the child to explore it if they wish. The child may point to the card or touch the sweater to begin.
2. Demonstrate using two fingers to grasp the rim of the button while gently holding the fabric next to it.
3. Encourage the child to pull the large button the rest of the way through the buttonhole, or point to the button for you to help steady the fabric.


* **Adult supervision needed**: Yes
* **Safety note**: Cardigan buttons can detach if pulled forcefully, presenting a mouthing and choking hazard; inspect all buttons before use and watch constantly.
* **Therapist reminder**: Check with your therapist before trying this.
* **When to stop**: Stop immediately if the child shows frustration, tiredness, or pulls their hands away.

### Activity 5: Pillow Squeeze Station

* **Title**: Pillow Squeeze Station
* **Basis**: Observation
* **Report quote**: "squeezing into tight spaces"
* **Triggers checked**:
* Need for deep pressure: Supported safely using ordinary, loose cushions without restricting movement.
* Wrapping/restraining body: Strictly avoided; the child controls all pressure and is never wrapped or wedged into an enclosed space.
* Messy textures: Clean, soft fabric cushions.
* Loud settings: Quiet home setting.
* Unsettled when feet leave the ground: Child remains sitting firmly on a floor rug with feet on the floor.


* **Materials**: Two standard bed pillows, one floor rug, one drawn picture card showing two pillows.
* **Duration**: 2 to 3 minutes.
* **Steps**:
1. Place the two bed pillows on the floor rug, show the picture card of two pillows, and invite the child to come sit between or near them if they wish. The child may point to the card to agree.
2. Model hugging one pillow tightly against your own chest to show a firm squeeze, then place the pillow in front of the child.
3. Invite the child to hug one pillow firmly with their arms or press down on top of both pillows using both hands while keeping their feet on the floor.


* **Adult supervision needed**: Yes
* **Safety note**: Soft pillows can cover the nose and mouth; ensure the child keeps their face completely clear of the pillows to avoid suffocation.
* **Therapist reminder**: Check with your therapist before trying this.
* **When to stop**: Stop immediately at any sign of tiredness, breathing difficulty, or distress.
